## Turn needs into verifiable behavior

Requirements describe capabilities and constraints that matter to stakeholders. Functional requirements state what a system should do; quality attributes describe properties such as reliability, security, and performance. A use case captures interactions and goals, while acceptance criteria make expected behavior testable.

Design decomposes the system into components with responsibilities and interfaces. An interface contract clarifies inputs, outputs, errors, and guarantees so teams can work independently. Architecture records should explain important decisions and tradeoffs, not just reproduce diagrams.

## Manage tradeoffs explicitly

Technical debt is a future cost created by a shortcut or design choice; not every compromise is harmful if it is deliberate and understood. Revisit decisions as constraints change, and include migration and operational costs in design discussions. Validate requirements with users and update them when evidence changes.
