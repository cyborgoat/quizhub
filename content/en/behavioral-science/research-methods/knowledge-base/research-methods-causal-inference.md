## Define the causal question

A causal question compares outcomes under different interventions or exposures for a clearly defined population and time. The counterfactual outcome under an unobserved alternative cannot usually be seen for the same person, so a design must support a credible comparison group.

Random assignment can balance confounders on average, while observational studies require assumptions and methods to address differences between groups. A confounder is a common cause of both the exposure and outcome; adjusting for the wrong variable can introduce bias, especially when conditioning on a collider or a post-treatment variable.

## Match conclusions to assumptions

Use a causal diagram or explicit model to clarify which variables are causes, mediators, or selection factors. Check how results change under plausible assumptions, report uncertainty, and distinguish association from identified causal effects. No single adjustment procedure removes the need to justify the design and assumptions.
