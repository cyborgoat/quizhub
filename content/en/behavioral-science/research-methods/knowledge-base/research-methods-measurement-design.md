## Turn constructs into evidence

A construct is an idea such as stress, trust, or skill; a measure is the procedure used to observe an indicator of it. Operational definitions make that choice explicit. Validity concerns whether evidence supports the intended interpretation and use, while reliability concerns consistency. A measure may be consistent yet fail to capture the construct of interest.

Study design should follow the question. Sampling determines which populations results can describe; random assignment and random sampling serve different purposes. Survey wording, response options, timing, and administration can influence answers, so pilot measures and inspect missingness and nonresponse.

## Check comparability and ethics

Compare measures across groups only when they function sufficiently similarly for the intended interpretation. Protect participants, minimize unnecessary data collection, and explain consent and privacy practices. Report limitations and uncertainty so readers can judge how far the findings travel.
