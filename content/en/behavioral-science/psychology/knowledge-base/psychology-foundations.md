## Study mind and behavior scientifically

Psychology investigates behavior and mental processes using multiple methods. A theory helps organize observations and generate testable predictions; a result supports a claim only to the extent that the design and measurements justify it. Correlation describes association but does not, by itself, establish that one variable caused another.

Perception, attention, memory, emotion, learning, and social context interact. A person’s behavior can reflect both individual history and the situation. Avoid treating one observation or a popular label as a complete explanation.

## Interpret evidence carefully

Measurement is an operational choice: researchers define how a construct will be observed, and different measures may capture different aspects of it. Consider sampling, context, alternative explanations, and uncertainty. Replication and converging methods strengthen confidence, while a finding should not be generalized beyond the people and settings studied without evidence.
