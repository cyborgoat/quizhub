## Keep a transparent research record

Responsible research separates decisions made before observing outcomes from exploratory analyses prompted by the data. Preregistration can make planned hypotheses and methods visible, but it does not prohibit exploration; report deviations and label exploratory work clearly. Preserve analytic choices, data transformations, and exclusions so readers can understand how results were produced.

Protect participants and data throughout a project. Collect only information needed for a justified purpose, use appropriate access controls, and follow relevant consent, oversight, retention, and sharing requirements. Document data and code sufficiently for collaborators to interpret and, where possible, reproduce the analysis.

## Share responsibility fairly

Discuss contributions, authorship, conflicts of interest, and review responsibilities openly. Peer review can improve a manuscript but cannot guarantee correctness. Null or uncertain findings are useful when methods and limitations are reported honestly.
