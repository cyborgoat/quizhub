## Make evidence usable without overstating it

Scientific work produces evidence with assumptions, measurement limits, and uncertainty. Separate observations from interpretations, state what a result can and cannot establish, and make uncertainty visible to decision-makers. A single study rarely settles a broad question; compare methods and findings across relevant evidence.

Clear communication is audience-aware but should not distort the underlying result. Define technical terms, give context for comparisons, and explain the practical meaning of uncertainty. When evidence is incomplete, describe what is known, what remains uncertain, and what additional information could change the decision.

## Build reliable collaborative practice

Teams benefit from explicit roles, shared documentation, careful handoffs, and respectful challenge. Raise safety, quality, and integrity concerns through suitable channels and document important decisions. Collaboration works best when credit, responsibility, and access to resources are handled transparently.
