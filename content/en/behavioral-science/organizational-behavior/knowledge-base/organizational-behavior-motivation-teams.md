## Motivation has several sources

People may act because work is interesting, meaningful, or aligned with their goals, and they may also respond to pay, recognition, or consequences. These motives can coexist. A reward can clarify priorities, but poorly designed incentives may shift attention toward the measured target and away from quality or cooperation.

Goals work best when people understand the desired outcome, have the resources and ability to pursue it, and receive feedback. Difficult goals should not encourage unsafe shortcuts or concealment. Autonomy, competence, and connection can support sustained engagement when the work allows them.

## Coordination is part of performance

Teams need shared expectations, role clarity, and reliable ways to exchange information. Conflict about ideas can improve decisions when handled respectfully; personal attacks and unresolved process conflict can obstruct work. Make dependencies visible, agree on decision ownership, and reflect on how the team will learn from outcomes.
