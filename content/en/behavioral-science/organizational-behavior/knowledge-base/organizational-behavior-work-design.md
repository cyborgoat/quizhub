## Design jobs around demands and resources

Work design concerns how tasks, responsibilities, relationships, and decision authority are organized. High demands are not automatically harmful, but sustained demands without sufficient time, tools, support, or control can strain people and reduce quality. Autonomy and meaningful feedback can help people adapt and take ownership, provided responsibilities are clear.

A job-design change should consider the whole workflow. Moving one task may shift hidden work to another role or create new handoffs. Ask affected workers where delays and errors occur, map dependencies, and test changes at a manageable scale.

## Make change learnable

Organizational change is easier to navigate when people understand what is changing, why it is needed, and how their work will be affected. Provide channels for questions, practical support, and a way to surface unintended effects. Monitor outcomes and revise the plan; communication alone cannot compensate for missing resources or conflicting incentives.
