## Learners vary; supports can respond

Learners bring different prior knowledge, language resources, goals, and experiences. These differences affect how demanding a task feels, but they do not justify assuming fixed ability from a single performance or preferred learning style. Use accessible materials, clear goals, and more than one way to engage with important ideas.

Working memory is limited, especially when material is unfamiliar. Clear sequencing, examples, and chunking can reduce unnecessary load. Once a learner has a useful foundation, gradually increase complexity and ask them to explain decisions rather than only imitate steps.

## Build transfer deliberately

Transfer means applying knowledge beyond the exact situation in which it was learned. Vary examples, compare cases, and ask learners to identify which principle applies and why. Provide enough retrieval and feedback to reveal misunderstandings, then revisit ideas over time. Do not equate fluent performance in one familiar task with broad mastery.
