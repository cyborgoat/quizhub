## Begin with the outcome

Instructional design connects goals, learning activities, and assessment. Specify what a learner should be able to do, decide what evidence would demonstrate it, and then choose practice that prepares learners to produce that evidence. This alignment makes it easier to find gaps between teaching and assessment.

Break complex skills into manageable steps without losing the meaningful whole. Worked examples can help novices notice a procedure; gradually remove support as competence grows. Retrieval, spaced practice, and feedback give learners repeated chances to strengthen and correct knowledge.

## Design for the actual learners

Account for prior knowledge, language, accessibility, and the demands of each task. Reduce avoidable distractions, but do not remove the productive effort needed to reason. Pilot activities and use learner work to revise instructions, timing, and supports rather than assuming a plan works equally well for everyone.
