## Assessment as evidence

Assessment is useful when its evidence supports a clear decision. Formative checks help instructors and learners adjust while learning is underway; summative assessments judge achievement at a defined point. A task should align with the intended learning outcome: recall questions cannot by themselves establish that someone can perform a complex procedure.

Validity concerns the interpretation and use of results, while reliability concerns consistency. Consistent scores can still measure the wrong thing. Rubrics make criteria visible, and rater calibration with examples can improve scoring agreement.

## Feedback that supports action

Useful feedback describes the work in relation to a goal and gives a feasible next step. It should arrive when the learner can still use it. A quiz can serve both assessment and practice when learners retrieve ideas, inspect responses, and correct misunderstandings.
